PHYSIOLOGY STUDY CORPUS

homeostasis is the maintenance of a relatively stable internal environment.
negative feedback reduces a change and moves a variable toward its set point.
positive feedback amplifies a change until a specific event is complete.
membranes separate fluid compartments and regulate movement between compartments.
diffusion moves particles down a concentration gradient.
osmosis is the movement of water across a selectively permeable membrane.
facilitated diffusion uses a membrane protein but does not directly require cellular energy.
active transport uses energy to move a substance against its electrochemical gradient.
the sodium potassium pump moves sodium out of a cell and potassium into a cell.
bulk transport moves large materials by endocytosis or exocytosis.
the resting membrane potential depends on ion gradients and selective membrane permeability.
graded potentials vary in size and decrease with distance.
an action potential is an all or none change in membrane voltage.
voltage gated sodium channels open during the rising phase of an action potential.
voltage gated potassium channels help restore the membrane toward its resting voltage.
the refractory period limits the frequency of action potentials and supports one way propagation.
the synapse is a junction where a neuron communicates with another cell.
calcium entry into a presynaptic terminal triggers neurotransmitter release.
acetylcholinesterase breaks down acetylcholine in the synaptic cleft.
the autonomic nervous system regulates involuntary visceral functions.
the sympathetic division supports activity associated with fight or flight.
the parasympathetic division supports rest digestion and energy conservation.
neurotransmitters produce effects by binding to receptors on a target cell.
ligand gated channels open when a chemical messenger binds to them.
muscle fibers contain myofibrils made of repeating sarcomeres.
actin and myosin interact to produce skeletal muscle contraction.
the neuromuscular junction connects a motor neuron to a skeletal muscle fiber.
acetylcholine released at the neuromuscular junction binds receptors on the motor end plate.
the action potential travels along the sarcolemma and into the t tubules.
the sarcoplasmic reticulum stores calcium used for muscle contraction.
the dihydropyridine receptor is a voltage sensor in the t tubule membrane.
DHP is the common abbreviation for the dihydropyridine receptor.
DHP activates RYR during excitation contraction coupling and helps release calcium.
the ryanodine receptor releases calcium from the sarcoplasmic reticulum.
RYR is the common abbreviation for the ryanodine receptor.
the dihydropyridine receptor activates the ryanodine receptor during excitation contraction coupling.
calcium binds troponin and moves tropomyosin away from actin binding sites.
atp binding and hydrolysis power cycling of myosin cross bridges.
calcium is returned to the sarcoplasmic reticulum during muscle relaxation.
motor units determine how many muscle fibers are activated by a motor neuron.
slow oxidative fibers resist fatigue and support endurance activity.
fast glycolytic fibers produce high force but fatigue more quickly.
smooth muscle does not use sarcomeres arranged like skeletal muscle.
vision begins when light is focused onto photoreceptors in the retina.
photoreceptors convert light into changes in membrane potential.
rods are sensitive in dim light and cones support color vision.
the cochlea converts sound vibrations into neural signals.
the vestibular system detects head movement and contributes to balance.
the heart has right and left sides that support pulmonary and systemic circulation.
the right ventricle pumps blood toward the lungs.
the left ventricle pumps blood into the systemic circulation.
cardiac valves maintain one way movement of blood through the heart.
the cardiac cycle includes filling and ejection phases.
cardiac output equals heart rate multiplied by stroke volume.
the sinoatrial node normally initiates the heartbeat.
the atrioventricular node delays conduction before the signal enters the ventricles.
an electrocardiogram records electrical activity associated with cardiac excitation.
arteries carry blood away from the heart and veins return blood toward the heart.
capillaries are thin vessels where exchange occurs between blood and tissues.
blood pressure depends on cardiac output and systemic vascular resistance.
baroreceptors detect changes in arterial stretch and help regulate blood pressure.
blood plasma is the fluid portion of blood.
red blood cells transport oxygen using hemoglobin.
white blood cells participate in immune defense.
platelets and clotting proteins contribute to hemostasis.
the kidneys regulate water electrolytes acid base balance and waste removal.
the nephron is the functional unit of the kidney.
filtration begins when pressure moves fluid from glomerular capillaries into bowman's capsule.
tubular reabsorption returns useful substances from tubular fluid to the blood.
tubular secretion moves selected substances from blood into tubular fluid.
antidiuretic hormone increases water reabsorption in the collecting ducts.
ADH is the common abbreviation for antidiuretic hormone.
the respiratory system moves air and exchanges oxygen and carbon dioxide.
alveoli provide a large surface area for gas exchange.
surfactant reduces surface tension and helps prevent alveolar collapse.
oxygen diffuses from alveoli into pulmonary capillary blood.
carbon dioxide diffuses from blood into alveolar air.
ventilation is the movement of air into and out of the lungs.
perfusion is blood flow through pulmonary capillaries.
the ventilation perfusion ratio affects the efficiency of gas exchange.
the digestive system breaks food into absorbable molecules.
the enteric nervous system helps coordinate digestive tract activity.
carbohydrates are digested into simple sugars.
proteins are digested into amino acids and small peptides.
lipids are digested into fatty acids and monoglycerides.
the endocrine system uses hormones to coordinate distant target cells.
water soluble hormones usually bind receptors on the cell membrane.
lipid soluble hormones often cross the membrane and bind intracellular receptors.
the hypothalamus links nervous and endocrine regulation.
the pituitary gland releases hormones that regulate other endocrine organs.
insulin lowers blood glucose by promoting glucose uptake and storage.
glucagon raises blood glucose by promoting glucose release into the blood.
the reproductive system produces gametes and supports reproduction.
the hypothalamus and pituitary regulate reproductive endocrine axes.
fertilization occurs when a sperm cell and an oocyte combine genetic material.
physiology connects cellular mechanisms with the function of tissues organs and organ systems.

ADDITIONAL PHYSIOLOGY CONTEXT

homeostasis depends on sensors control centers and effectors working together.
a controlled variable is measured by a receptor and compared with a desired range.
the response of a control system depends on the stimulus the sensor and the effector.
temperature blood glucose blood pressure and blood calcium are examples of regulated variables.
compartments of the body include intracellular fluid and extracellular fluid.
extracellular fluid includes interstitial fluid and plasma.
electrochemical gradients combine concentration differences with electrical forces.
ion channels may be leak gated ligand gated or voltage gated.
membrane permeability determines which ions most strongly influence membrane voltage.
depolarization makes the inside of a cell less negative.
hyperpolarization makes the inside of a cell more negative.
threshold is the membrane voltage at which an action potential is triggered.
action potentials propagate without decreasing in size along an excitable membrane.
saltatory conduction allows an action potential to move rapidly between nodes of ranvier.
myelin electrically insulates an axon and increases conduction speed.
excitatory postsynaptic potentials increase the chance of a postsynaptic action potential.
inhibitory postsynaptic potentials decrease the chance of a postsynaptic action potential.
temporal summation combines repeated signals from one presynaptic source.
spatial summation combines signals arriving from multiple synapses.
the adrenal medulla releases epinephrine and norepinephrine during sympathetic activation.
many autonomic pathways use acetylcholine at the preganglionic synapse.
most sympathetic postganglionic neurons release norepinephrine at their target organs.
parasympathetic postganglionic neurons commonly release acetylcholine.
receptor type and target tissue determine the effect of a neurotransmitter.

skeletal muscle contraction begins with excitation of a motor neuron.
an end plate potential is produced when acetylcholine activates receptors on the muscle fiber.
the muscle action potential travels across the sarcolemma and down the t tubules.
the t tubule voltage change is detected by the dihydropyridine receptor.
the dihydropyridine receptor communicates with the ryanodine receptor in the sarcoplasmic reticulum.
ryanodine receptor opening increases cytosolic calcium near the contractile proteins.
cross bridge cycling continues while calcium exposes binding sites and atp is available.
the power stroke moves the thin filament toward the center of the sarcomere.
muscle tension depends on cross bridge number and the length of the sarcomere.
isometric contraction produces force without changing muscle length.
isotonic contraction changes muscle length while producing force.
frequency summation occurs when a second stimulus arrives before complete relaxation.
tetanus is a sustained high tension produced by rapid repeated stimulation.
muscle fatigue can involve energy depletion altered ion gradients and reduced excitation.
creatine phosphate provides a rapid short term source for regenerating atp.
aerobic metabolism supports longer activity when oxygen delivery is sufficient.

the cardiac muscle action potential has a prolonged plateau phase.
the plateau helps prevent cardiac tetanus and allows the heart to fill and pump rhythmically.
autorhythmic cells generate spontaneous electrical activity.
the pacemaker potential gradually reaches threshold in autorhythmic cells.
the cardiac conduction sequence is sinoatrial node atrioventricular node bundle branches and purkinje fibers.
preload describes ventricular filling and is related to end diastolic volume.
afterload is the pressure the ventricle must overcome to eject blood.
contractility describes the strength of contraction at a given preload.
stroke volume is end diastolic volume minus end systolic volume.
the frank starling relationship links increased filling with increased contraction force within limits.
sympathetic stimulation increases heart rate and contractility.
parasympathetic stimulation mainly slows the heart through the sinoatrial and atrioventricular nodes.
mean arterial pressure is influenced by cardiac output and systemic vascular resistance.
arterioles are important resistance vessels that regulate tissue blood flow.
vasoconstriction increases resistance and vasodilation decreases resistance.
local metabolites can increase blood flow to active tissues.
capillary filtration and reabsorption are influenced by hydrostatic and osmotic forces.

the nephron includes a renal corpuscle a proximal tubule a loop of henle a distal tubule and a collecting duct.
glomerular filtration is influenced by hydrostatic pressure and the filtration barrier.
the proximal tubule reabsorbs much of the filtered water and solute load.
the loop of henle helps create a medullary osmotic gradient.
the distal tubule and collecting duct adjust electrolyte and water balance.
aldosterone increases sodium reabsorption and potassium secretion in selected nephron segments.
antidiuretic hormone increases collecting duct water permeability.
the renin angiotensin aldosterone system supports blood pressure and extracellular fluid volume.
the kidneys help regulate blood ph by excreting acid and conserving bicarbonate.
respiratory and renal systems work together to regulate acid base balance.

air moves because of pressure differences between the atmosphere and alveoli.
inspiration occurs when thoracic volume increases and alveolar pressure falls.
expiration at rest is mainly caused by elastic recoil of the lungs and chest wall.
lung compliance describes how easily the lungs expand.
surfactant improves compliance by reducing the tendency of alveoli to collapse.
oxygen is transported mainly bound to hemoglobin.
carbon dioxide is transported mainly as bicarbonate in the blood.
carbon dioxide and hydrogen ions influence hemoglobin oxygen binding.
central and peripheral chemoreceptors help regulate ventilation.
the medulla and pons coordinate the pattern of breathing.

the liver processes absorbed nutrients and produces bile.
the pancreas releases digestive enzymes and bicarbonate into the small intestine.
the small intestine is the primary site of nutrient digestion and absorption.
the large intestine absorbs water and forms feces.
the endocrine response is often slower than a neural response but can last longer.
steroid hormones are synthesized from cholesterol and often alter gene transcription.
peptide hormones commonly activate second messenger pathways.
the hypothalamus controls pituitary function through releasing and inhibiting hormones.
the anterior pituitary releases tropic hormones that regulate target endocrine glands.
the posterior pituitary releases oxytocin and antidiuretic hormone made in the hypothalamus.
thyroid hormones increase the metabolic activity of many tissues.
cortisol supports metabolism and helps coordinate longer term stress responses.
insulin and glucagon provide opposing regulation of blood glucose.
negative feedback commonly regulates endocrine axes.
